Taekwondo is mainly a striking art. Kicks delivered from a mobile stance are its best-known feature, and hand techniques, blocks and stances are taught from the first lessons. Each organization and school defines its own syllabus.

## Technique groups

- **Kicks:** front, roundhouse, side, back, axe and hook kicks, and jumping and turning variations.
- **Hand techniques:** straight punches, knife-hand, ridge-hand, back-fist and palm-heel strikes.
- **Blocks:** low, middle and high blocks, inward and outward blocks, and other deflections.
- **Stances:** walking, front, back, sitting and sparring stances.

## Patterns

A pattern is a fixed sequence of techniques performed alone. The terms *hyeong*, *pumse* (poomsae) and *teul* (tul) all mean "form" or "pattern". *Hyeong* is used mainly in earlier traditional Taekwondo, *poomsae* in the Kukkiwon and World Taekwondo tradition, and *tul* in the ITF tradition.[^1]

| Tradition | Colour-belt patterns | Black-belt patterns |
|---|---|---|
| Kukkiwon and World Taekwondo | Eight Taegeuk forms, Il Jang to Pal Jang | Nine Yudanja forms: Koryo, Keumgang, Taebaek, Pyongwon, Sipjin, Jitae, Cheonkwon, Hansoo and Ilyeo[^1] |
| ITF | Chang-Hon tul beginning with Cheon-Ji, Dan-Gun and Do-San | Later Chang-Hon tul through Tong-Il; the official ITF syllabus contains 24 patterns[^2] |

Each Taegeuk form corresponds to one trigram of the *I Ching*. The Taegeuk forms replaced the earlier Palgwe forms, which also followed the eight trigrams, in 1971.[^1] Sources differ on how many ITF patterns exist at a given time: Go-Dang was removed from the ITF syllabus in the early 1980s and replaced by Juche, so lists of ITF patterns vary in count.[^1]

## Sparring

Sparring, called *kyorugi* or *matsogi*, is part of training in most schools. Rules differ between World Taekwondo, ITF groups and independent schools. See [[Sparring and protective equipment]].
