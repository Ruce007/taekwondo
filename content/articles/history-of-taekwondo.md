Modern Taekwondo has no single, universally recognized founder. It grew out of several Korean schools, called kwans, and later split into separate international organizations. Dates and credit are contested in some places, and this article says so where it matters.

## Before 1945

Older Korean martial practices such as taekkyeon (also spelled taekkyon) existed before modern Taekwondo.[^1] Korea was under Japanese rule until 1945, and several men who later founded kwans had trained in Japanese karate or judo, in Chinese martial arts or in taekkyeon. Examples include [[Lee Won-kuk]], [[Hwang Kee]], [[Ro Byung-jick]] and [[Chun Sang-sup]].[^2][^4][^5]

## The original kwans (1944 to 1955)

After liberation in 1945, instructors opened schools that blended their different training backgrounds. Founding dates differ between sources, and each kwan page notes the differences.

|---|---|---|

| [[Song Moo Kwan]] | [[Ro Byung-jick]] | 20 March 1944, Kaesong[^2] |
| [[Moo Duk Kwan]] | [[Hwang Kee]] | 1945[^2] |
| [[Chang Moo Kwan]] | [[Yun Byung-in]] | 1946, at a YMCA Kwon Bup club[^2] |

| [[Oh Do Kwan]] | [[Choi Hong-hi]] and [[Nam Tae-hi]] | Mid-1950s[^2] |

## Choosing a name (1955 to 1965)

Accounts of how the name Taekwondo was chosen differ:

- The ITF says a board of politicians, military and business leaders, historians and instructors approved the name Taekwon-Do on 11 April 1955, with President Syngman Rhee's approval.[^3]
- Kwan leaders began discussing a unified art in 1955, with Choi Hong-hi advocating the name, and the nine original kwans agreed on it in 1959.[^1]
- The name became official under the Korea Taekwondo Association on 5 August 1965.[^4]


## Diverging organizations (1966 to 1973)

- **22 March 1966:** [[Choi Hong-hi]] founded the [[International Taekwon-Do Federation]] in Seoul.[^7]
- **1972:** Choi went into exile in Canada.[^9] The Korea Taekwondo Association's central training hall opened in Seoul, and it was officially named the [[Kukkiwon]] on 6 February 1973.[^8]
- **28 May 1973:** The World Taekwondo Federation was established at an inaugural meeting at the Kukkiwon, with 35 representatives.[^6]

## Olympic recognition and growth (1980 to today)

The International Olympic Committee recognized the World Taekwondo Federation on 17 July 1980. Taekwondo was a demonstration sport at the 1988 and 1992 Games and became an official medal sport at the 2000 Games in Sydney. The federation was renamed [[World Taekwondo]] in June 2017.[^6] See [[Taekwondo at the Olympics]].

The ITF divided after 2001 and 2002 into separate groups, commonly called ITF-Vienna, ITF-Poland and ITF-UK.[^7]
