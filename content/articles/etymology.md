The word **Taekwondo** is written 태권도 in Hangul, the Korean alphabet, and 跆拳道 in hanja, the Chinese characters used in Korean. It is commonly analyzed as three parts:[^1]

| Part | Hangul (Korean) | Hanja (Chinese characters) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tae | 태 | 跆 | To strike or stomp with the foot |
| Kwon | 권 | 拳 | The fist, or to strike with the hand |
| Do | 도 | 道 | The way, path or discipline |

It is therefore often translated as "the way of the foot and the fist".

## An earlier name: Tae Soo Do

An earlier name, Tae Soo Do (태수도), was written 跆手道 in hanja, with 跆 (tae), 手 (su, "hand") and 道 (do). [[Choi Hong-hi]] replaced 手 with 拳 (kwon, "fist") to form the name Taekwondo.[^1] The page [[History of Taekwondo]] covers the disputed dates for when the name was adopted.

## Chinese characters and the naming

The names that came before Taekwondo were also formed from Chinese characters: Tang Soo Do is 唐手道, the Korean reading of the characters for karate, and Tae Soo Do is 跆手道.[^1] The change to Taekwondo was a one-character substitution, 手 ("hand") becoming 拳 ("fist"), which Choi advocated.[^1]

Whether the 1955 records themselves used hanja is disputed. Choi's own accounts date the naming to 11 April 1955.[^1] The researcher Lee Kyung-myung dates the first recorded adoption of the name to an advisers' meeting of the [[Chung Do Kwan]] on 19 December 1955. In his account the minutes are written mostly in Hangul, and the name entered in them is a two-syllable form, 태拳 (the Hangul 태 followed by the hanja 拳), not the full three-syllable 태권도. He also disputes Choi's statement that Rhee wrote the name in Chinese characters, saying the president's calligraphy was in Hangul.[^2] The kwan leaders agreed on the name taekwondo in 1959.[^1]

## Spellings

The art appears in English as Taekwondo, Tae Kwon Do and, in the ITF tradition, Taekwon-Do. The spellings refer to the same word. This site uses "Taekwondo" except where an organization's own name or usage is being quoted.
