ATA Martial Arts, formerly the American Taekwondo Association, is a Taekwondo organization founded in 1969 in Omaha, Nebraska, by Haeng Ung Lee. Its headquarters moved to Little Rock, Arkansas, in 1977.[^1][^3] Its licensed schools teach Songahm Taekwondo, a curriculum that Lee introduced in 1983. "Songahm" means "pine tree and rock", a name given by Lee's brother Soon Ho Lee.[^2][^3]

## Founder

Haeng Ung Lee was born on 20 July 1936 in Manchuria and began martial arts training in 1953. He served as a martial arts instructor in the Republic of Korea Army, founded a private Taekwondo school in Osan, South Korea, in 1959, and immigrated to the United States in 1962. He died on 5 October 2000.[^2] After his death his brother Soon Ho Lee led the ATA, followed by another brother, In Ho Lee, in 2011. The title of Presiding Grand Master went to G. K. Lee in 2019 and to M. K. Lee in 2022.[^1]

## Size and structure

The Encyclopedia of Arkansas reports more than 300,000 members worldwide, with more than 900 licensed locations in 25 countries in 2019 and about 1,100 by 2024.[^1] The Songahm Taekwondo Federation, founded in 1984, serves South America, and the World Traditional Taekwondo Union, formed in 1990, covers the rest of the world.[^3] The ATA World Expo was held in Little Rock each year until 2019, after which it was renamed the ATA Worlds and moved to Phoenix, Arizona.[^1]

## Curriculum and ranks

Songahm Taekwondo covers forms (poomsae), sparring, one-steps, self-defense, board breaking, weapons and leadership. Students advance through a colored-belt series and a black-belt series with nine ranks each, and the 18 forms performed in sequence are called the Songahm Star.[^3]

## Relationship to World Taekwondo, the Kukkiwon and the ITF

The ATA is an independent organization with its own curriculum, rank structure and licensing system. Its website, the Encyclopedia of Arkansas and Wikipedia do not describe it as a member of, or certified by, [[World Taekwondo]], the [[Kukkiwon]] or the [[International Taekwon-Do Federation]]; Lee described Songahm as a response to flaws in the Japanese-influenced Taekwondo commonly taught in the United States.[^2][^3][^4] In the United States, the national governing body recognized by World Taekwondo is [[USA Taekwondo]], a separate organization.
