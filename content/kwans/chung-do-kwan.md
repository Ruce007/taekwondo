The Chung Do Kwan was founded by [[Lee Won-kuk]] in Korea in 1944. One source lists September 1944; another gives c. 1944.[^1][^2]

## Leaders and students

Son Duk-sung became head of the kwan, and Uhm Woon-kyu succeeded him in 1959. Yong Taek Chung opened its first branch outside Korea, in Tokyo. Jhoon Rhee is listed among its graduates.[^1]

## Role in the name and unification

The Chung Do Kwan was one of the nine original kwans that formed the basis of the Korea Taekwondo Association and the [[Kukkiwon]]. Wikipedia says Choi Hong-hi proposed the name Taekwondo in 1955 and that the Chung Do Kwan and [[Oh Do Kwan]] adopted it at his direction.[^1] See [[History of Taekwondo]] for the different accounts of the naming.

## Today

The Korean organization still exists and issues dan certificates from its headquarters in Seoul. Sources differ on its character: one passage calls it a fraternal social club that is no longer a martial arts style, while others say it follows the Kukkiwon curriculum, and some affiliated schools still teach older forms such as Pyong-Ahn and Palgwae.[^1]

## Disputed claims

The same source calls the Chung Do Kwan both the "second oldest" kwan established after the occupation and "the first Kwan to open in Korea". It also notes that some claims on the topic are not cited.[^1] This site therefore does not call it the oldest.
