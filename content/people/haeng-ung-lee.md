Haeng Ung Lee was born on 20 July 1936 in Manchuria and moved to Korea after the Second World War. He began martial arts training in 1953 and earned a first-degree black belt in 1954.[^1][^2][^3]

## Career in Korea

Lee entered the Republic of Korea Army in 1956 and served three years as a martial arts instructor for special troops.[^1][^2] In 1959 he opened a private school in Osan, including a branch at the nearby U.S. air base, where he met Richard Reed.[^1]

## The United States and the ATA

Reed sponsored Lee's move to the United States in 1962, and they opened a school in Omaha, Nebraska.[^1] Lee founded the American Taekwondo Association in 1969 and became a U.S. citizen in 1973; the [[ATA Martial Arts]] moved to Little Rock, Arkansas, in 1977.[^1][^2] In 1983 he introduced Songahm Taekwondo, and in 1990 the ATA's Master's Council awarded him 9th dan and the title Grand Master.[^1][^2] He died of lung cancer on 5 October 2000 and was later promoted posthumously to 10th dan, "Eternal Grand Master".[^1][^2][^3]

## Lineage

No published source names Lee's teachers or his kwan, and none connects him to the original kwans described in [[History of Taekwondo]]. One ATA-affiliated school's history page claims that [[Choi Hong-hi]] taught him the first Chang-hun forms in 1968; no independent source confirms it. The [[Kukkiwon]] and Korean sources do not mention his rank, and his 9th dan came from the ATA's own council. Lee described Songahm as a response to flaws in the Japanese-influenced Taekwondo commonly taught in the United States.[^1]

## Honors reported by the ATA

The ATA reports a 1987 training camp at Haein Temple, a 1991 monument at the Choong Moo Memorial Center, a 1996 Highest Profile Award and a 1997 KBS Cultural Promotion Award in Korea.[^4] These have not been independently confirmed.
